# Strategy Page Content Best Practices

## What Great Looks Like

- **Anchor the play in its Wardley context.** Start with the strategic problem, then show how the play shifts the landscape and which component stages are involved. For example, [Cooperation](/strategies/accelerators/cooperation) treats collaboration as a way to speed evolution, not as generic teamwork. [Directed Investment](/strategies/attacking/directed-investment) shows where bold bets make sense.
- **Explain the strategy from more than one angle.** Cover the definition, why it matters, how it works, and common variants. Strong pages layer "what/why/how" guidance with concrete patterns. For example, [Weak Signal (Horizon)](/strategies/positional/weak-signal-horizon) explains sensing loops, and [Alliances](/strategies/ecosystem/alliances) separates alliances from broader cooperation.
- **Tie moves to real landscapes.** Use concrete success stories, failures, and clearly labelled hypotheticals so readers can see the play under different conditions. Connect each story back to map position or ecosystem pressure.
- **Show leaders how to decide and act.** Do more than list steps. Explain leadership challenges, required capabilities, and ethical tensions. [Procrastination](/strategies/defensive/procrastination) shows the discipline of waiting. [Directed Investment](/strategies/attacking/directed-investment) focuses on portfolio stewardship and risk appetite.
- **Measure readiness and success.** Include assessment prompts, trigger conditions, and outcome metrics that match real decision points. Call out key signals, common readiness gaps, and how impact should appear in operations or markets.
- **Show interplay across strategies and patterns.** Explain how the play complements, conflicts with, or unlocks other moves and climatic patterns. Add short relationship notes, not just links, so strategists can see sequencing and counterplay options quickly.
- **End with original insights.** Use "Strategic Insights" for synthesis that adds something new: a mental model, timing nuance, or response pattern readers would not get from earlier summaries.

## Opportunities to Raise the Bar Further

- **Connect maps or visuals to decisions.** When you include diagrams or mind maps, add a short interpretation so readers know what to copy, adapt, or monitor in their own landscape.
- **Balance range with focus in storytelling.** Keep case studies tightly linked to the mechanism. Use contrasting examples to show boundaries, including where the play fails or when to hand off to an adjacent strategy.
- **Surface likely follow-on plays.** Close with practical next moves after adoption: who to partner with, which doctrines to reinforce, and what counterplay to expect.
